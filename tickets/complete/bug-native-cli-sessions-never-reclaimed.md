## What landed

The native CLI - the server's simplified call interface - hands out *sessions* rather than connections, and nothing watched them. A client that started a session, opened a transaction, changed the schema and then died left that transaction open for the life of the server, holding the catalog store's single write lock against every other session's schema change, each of which then failed with error 130103 after its lock wait. This adds the sweep that ends such a session, reading the clock and the in-use count `native-cli-session-liveness-clock` put on each session.

**`NativeServer.ReclaimIdleSessions(int idleSeconds)`** - new, public, returns how many sessions it stopped. Two steps, and the split is the substance. Under `lock (_nativeSessions)` it selects the sessions whose `IdleSeconds` exceeds the threshold (that property reports 0 while a call is in flight, so one comparison applies both conditions) and removes each from the dictionary; deciding and removing is one atomic step, so a session the sweep has chosen cannot take a call in between. Outside the lock it reads each removed session's open-transaction count for the log, calls the existing `StopNativeSession` in its own `try`, and writes one log line. Outside because stopping a session's process can wait half of `Engine.ProcessTerminationTimeout` - 15 s by default - and every operation of `NativeServer` takes that lock. `StopNativeSession` is what rolls the transaction back: it disposes the `ServerProcess`, which rolls back its open transactions and releases the store's write lock. No new rollback machinery.

**`src/engine/Server/Service/NativeSessionManager.cs`** - new public class beside `ConnectionManager`, with that class's shape copied: `IdleTimeInSeconds` of 300, `WatcherStopTimeoutInSeconds` of 10, two constructors (the second taking the threshold, refusing below 1 second), one background watcher thread, a `ManualResetEvent`, a wake period of a quarter of the threshold with a floor of a second, an idempotent `Dispose` with a bounded join, and guarded logging. A `NOTE:` at the top records that the thread shape is copied deliberately rather than extracted, and that a third such watcher is the condition for extracting one.

**`DataphorServiceHost.NativeSessionIdleTimeInSeconds`** - new host-object property, defaulting to `NativeSessionManager.IdleTimeInSeconds`, built into the manager in `Start` beside the native service and disposed in `Stop` before the `RemoteServer` and before the engine stops.

**No new error, no new error code.** A client returning to a reclaimed session gets the "Invalid session handle" `ArgumentException` the dictionary lookup already raises, wrapped by `NativeCLIUtility.WrapException`. The server log is the operator-visible record: one line per reclamation naming the session ID, user, client host name, idle seconds and open-transaction count.

`docs/architecture.md`, `docs/modernization-plan.md` (WS2, with a **Decisions for the native interface** block) and `docs/migration-notes.md` carry the result; the architecture sentence saying native sessions were covered by no watcher, and the remark on `RemoteSessionImplementation.Dispose` saying "What reclaims it, as of this version: nothing", were corrected because the change made them false.

Docs swept for stale "nothing reclaims a native session" claims - none left outside other tickets' own bodies. **Fixed in this pass (minor).**

- `src/engine/Server/Server.csproj`: the new `<Compile Include="Service\NativeSessionManager.cs" />` had been inserted between `DataphorService.cs` and `HandleManager.cs`, breaking the alphabetical order the rest of the list keeps. Moved after `HandleManager.cs`.

**Closed by measurement rather than left to inspection.** The handoff's first flagged gap was that the log line is only partly exercised - the suite runs with logging off, so the `String.Format` runs but nothing sees the line reach a file. Measured instead of tested: the reproduction case re-run with `DATAPHOR_TEST_LOGGING=1` and the instance's `Instance/Log/Dataphor.log` read. It holds `Native session reclaimed due to inactivity: SessionID=e4137e08-9778-44f8-915e-34dae853c8e5, UserID=Admin, HostName=AbandonedNativeClient, IdleSeconds=2, TransactionCount=1`. Every field is populated, `TransactionCount=1` is the operator-useful part the plan claims for it, and `IdleSeconds=2` confirms the strict `>` comparison at a one-second threshold. No test added for it: the suite has no precedent for reading a server log, building that machinery to pin a log line's wording is more cost than the line is worth, and the format call is already exercised by every passing run.

**Recorded as a tripwire, not a ticket.** `NativeSessionManager`'s constructor checks only the floor of its threshold. A value above about 8,589,934 seconds (99 days) makes `_wakePeriodInSeconds * 1000` in `LifetimeWatcher` overflow `int`, so `WaitOne` is handed a negative time-out, throws, and the blanket `catch` there ends the watcher silently for the life of the process. Conditional and harmless today - the one plausible reason to ask for a threshold that large is to turn reclamation off, which is what happens - so a `NOTE:` at the constructor's guard, naming the bound `LocalServer` and `NativeCLISession` already validate their keep-alive intervals to (1..2,147,483) for the same millisecond overflow, and naming the condition for adding it. Left unfixed rather than corrected because `ConnectionManager` has the identical hole and the plan deliberately keeps the twin classes symmetric: bounding one and not the other would be the worse state, and bounding both means a range change on two documented public constructors for a case nobody reaches.

**Considered and declined.** Each of these is a judgment the handoff itself put on the agenda, and each was weighed against the code rather than waved through.

- **`ReclaimIdleSessions` public with no caller outside the assembly.** Kept public. It is exactly the shape of `RemoteServer.GetCurrentConnections`/`Relinquish`, which `ConnectionManager` calls from the same assembly and which are public for the same stated reason; the repository has no `InternalsVisibleTo` to use instead, confirmed by grep; and the migration note already describes it as public surface.
- **Its `idleSeconds < 1` guard duplicating the manager's.** Kept. It is a public method's own boundary check, and it is what makes relying on `IdleSeconds` reporting 0 for a busy session sound at that boundary and not only at the manager's.
- **`TransactionCountOf` being defensive beyond the facts.** Kept. Both branches of its guard are indeed unreachable with the product's own `ServerProcess` - `Process` is never null for a session in the table, and `TransactionCount` is a list-count read - but the field is typed `IServerProcess`, and the thing it is guarding against is exactly the defect being fixed: a throw while reading a detail of a log line would skip the stop that follows it.
- **The log line written after the stop where `ConnectionManager` writes its before.** Kept, and the divergence is correct: by that point the session is out of the table and nothing else will ever stop it, so a logging failure must not be able to skip the stop.
- **Three helpers duplicated from `AbandonedSessionCleanupTest`.** Kept, with the fixture's existing `NOTE:`. Each is a few lines carrying a failure message that names what *this* fixture drives, and extracting them would mean editing a fixture `port-abandoned-session-cleanup` has only just proven.
- **The roughly two-second window at the end of `ASessionWithACallInFlightIsNotReclaimed`.** The final handle read must land within two seconds of the long call's release or the session is a legitimate candidate again. Looked at moving the read outside the `using` so the manager's `Dispose` would stop the watcher first; that only shifts the same window from "release to read" to "release to `Dispose`" and does not remove it, so it was not done. The case's own comment already names the hazard, and the budget is microseconds of work against two seconds.
- **`NativeCLIService` carrying no pointer to what reclaims the sessions it hands out.** Not annotated. A reader asking that question is at `NativeServer`, where the sessions live and where the sweep and its remarks are; a note in an otherwise untouched forwarder is churn.

**No tickets filed and nothing routed to `blocked/`.** Deliberately, and so the site-claim grep was not needed: the one finding was a project-file ordering slip fixed in the pass, the one hazard found is conditional on a 99-day threshold and belongs in code as a tripwire by the review rules, and every other item on the handoff's agenda is a judgment that survived being checked. Nothing in the change is silent or contradictory about the specification, so there is no proposal for a human to accept.

**What is still carried by inspection rather than by a test**, unchanged from the handoff and re-read here rather than taken on trust: a session acquired between the decision and the stop is impossible because the removal is inside the lock (and the same property makes two overlapping sweeps safe, which the handoff did not claim); a brand-new session is never swept before its first call because its clock is stamped in `NativeSession`'s constructor; a client `StopSession` racing the sweep leaves `NativeCLISession.Dispose` propagating and `RemoteSessionImplementation.Dispose` logging a warning and releasing the local side; a session whose process will not stop delays the rest of its sweep; a reclamation overlapping server shutdown is covered by the dispose ordering in `Stop` and the guarded logging; and the stateless `Execute(NativeSessionInfo, ...)` overloads cannot be swept because their sessions never enter the table. The two keep-alive cases remain wall-clock judgments at a three-second threshold with a one-second ping, as their own remarks say.
