description: The server used to decide a client had gone away by subtracting two readings of the local clock, so a clock that jumped forward — the spring daylight-saving change, an operator correcting the time — made every connected client look idle at once and the server threw all of their work away. Elapsed time is now measured from a clock that only ever moves forward at one rate.

## What landed

Two places in the server decided something by subtracting one reading of `DateTime.Now` from another. Both now measure elapsed time from a monotonic source and compare elapsed against elapsed. The threshold, the budget and the boundary of each comparison are unchanged; only the clock behind them changed.

**Arm 1 — the connection idle watcher.** `RemoteServerConnection` no longer keeps `DateTime _lastActivityTime`. It keeps `long _lastActivityTicks` from `Environment.TickCount64`, stamped in the constructor and in `Ping` with `Volatile.Write`, and the public read is `public TimeSpan IdleTime`. `ConnectionManager` holds its threshold as a `TimeSpan` built once in the constructor, `RelinquishIdleConnections` takes no parameter any more (there is no instant for a caller to compute), and the sweep reads each connection's idle time once into a local, compares it strictly against the threshold — the boundary the wall-clock comparison had — and logs that same local as `IdleSeconds=<n>` where it used to log `LastActivity=<clock time>`.

**Arm 2 — the process-termination deadline.** `Engine.TerminateProcessThread` took its deadline from `DateTime.Now.AddTicks(ProcessTerminationTimeout.Ticks / 2)` and spun until `DateTime.Now > endTime`. It now starts a `Stopwatch` where that deadline used to be computed and compares `terminationWatch.Elapsed > terminationBudget`, the budget still half of `ProcessTerminationTimeout` and the comparison still strict.

`Stopwatch` in arm 2 and a plain tick count with `Volatile` in arm 1, deliberately: arm 2's deadline is a local read by one thread inside one method, while the connection clock is a field written by ping threads and read by the watcher thread — which is how `NativeSession` already does it.

`LastActivityTime` was replaced rather than kept beside the new measure: two clocks on one object is the trap that produced the bug, and the only thing the `DateTime` was still buying was one field of one log line. It is the ticket's only compatibility surface, source-level only — not on `IRemoteServerConnection`, not on a WCF contract, not reachable from D4 or a form document — and `docs/migration-notes.md` carries it. The remarks on `ConnectionManager`, `RemoteServerConnection` and `NativeSession`, and the *Reclaiming an abandoned session* section of `docs/modernization-plan.md` (including a new **Decisions** bullet), were corrected to match.

**Tests.** `ConnectionKeepAliveTest.TheSweepSparesAPingedConnectionAndReclaimsAnUnpingedOne` is the new regression case: at a ten-second threshold, a connection a `LocalServer` pings every second is spared by the very sweep that reclaims an unpinged control connection. `WaitForAPingToLand`, the fixture's existing helper, was converted from waiting for an instant to rise to waiting for the idle time to fall between two consecutive reads, since only a ping shortens it; its three call sites keep the coverage they had. No test for arm 2 — reaching that deadline needs a process whose thread will not stop when aborted, which this suite cannot produce — and none for a clock step, which cannot be produced in process at all: `TZ` plus `TimeZoneInfo.ClearCachedData()` does not move `TimeZoneInfo.Local` on Windows, and moving the machine clock is out of bounds for a test.

## Review findings

**What was checked.** The implement diff (`ticket(implement): bug-idle-clocks-use-wall-clock-time`) read before the handoff summary. The boundary algebra of both arms worked through rather than trusted: `lastActivity < now - threshold` is `idle > threshold` with the same strictness, and `DateTime.Now > endTime` is `Elapsed > budget` with the `Stopwatch` started exactly where `endTime` used to be computed and nothing between it and the loop that could restart it. `System.Diagnostics` is imported in `Engine.cs` and `Stopwatch` is unambiguous there, which is not obvious three lines above a `Debug.Debugger` that resolves to `Alphora.Dataphor.DAE.Debug` rather than to `System.Diagnostics`. The tree was grepped for `LastActivityTime` across source, docs, scripts and project files: nothing stale, and `IRemoteServerConnection` carries only `ConnectionName`, `Connect`, `Disconnect` and `Ping`, so no contract shape moved. The wall-clock survey was re-run independently (`grep -rn "DateTime.Now\|DateTime.UtcNow" --include=*.cs src/`) and every hit classified; no site that *decides* anything from a difference of two wall-clock readings is left in the server. The `Volatile` comment's claim about the 64-bit runtime was checked against the build rather than accepted: `Directory.Build.props` sets `net10.0` with no `PlatformTarget`, and the only projects that set one set `AnyCPU` with `Prefer32Bit=false`. `docs/architecture.md` was grepped for `wall clock`, `DateTime`, `monotonic` and `TickCount` — no hits, which is why its line 71 describing the 300-to-375-second window needed no edit; the migration-notes row and the plan section were read line by line against the code. Hygiene: the three files edited in this pass keep their tabs, CRLF endings and UTF-8 byte-order mark; they measure 245, 239 and 373 lines (`wc -l`).

**Verified by mutation rather than taken on trust.** The sweep's comparison was inverted to `<`, the solution rebuilt, and the new case run: it fails in 6 s on the pinged arm with its own named message, "The sweep reclaimed a connection the client was pinging every second, at an idle threshold of 10 s". Reverted, rebuilt, re-ran green. That run also showed something the handoff did not claim and a future reader should know: under the inversion the *control* connection was still reported reclaimed, at the first sweep, because its idle time was then below the threshold. So the control is not what catches an inverted comparison — the pinged arm is. The two arms are complementary rather than redundant: the control catches a watcher that never sweeps, and the pinged connection catches a comparison that is inverted or mis-scaled. The case is 13 to 14 s here, as the handoff measured.

**Fixed in this pass (minor).**

- `RemoteServerConnection.IdleTime` read the clock before the stamp. In that order a ping landing between the two reads moves the stamp past the clock reading already taken, and the property returns a negative `TimeSpan`. Nothing acted wrongly on one — the sweep spares a negative, and the test helper reads a fall as the ping it is — but a public elapsed-time property that can run backwards is not what a caller is promised, and the stamp-then-clock order makes it total for free. Reordered, with the reason at the site. No migration-notes row for it: the row that landed with the implement commit describes `IdleTime` as how long it is since the ping, which a never-negative value satisfies, and nothing could have depended on a sub-microsecond race.
- `ConnectionManager._idleTime` renamed `_idleThreshold`. The sweep's line read `if (idleTime > _idleTime)` — a measurement and a threshold under one name, at the one site the whole fix turns on.
- The remark on `SweepThresholdSeconds` said the watcher wakes "every 2.5 s here". `_wakePeriodInSeconds` is `Math.Max(1, idleTimeInSeconds / 4)`, integer division, so at a ten-second threshold it is 2 s. Corrected and the paragraph rewrapped.

**Recorded as a tripwire, not a ticket.** Whether to extract a shared monotonic-clock helper. `native-cli-session-liveness-clock`'s review set the condition "extract on the third site", but that condition lived only in that ticket's body, which is archived — the only "third site" condition in the code is `NativeSessionManager`'s, and it is about watcher *threads*, not clocks. So a reader at either stamp field met the cross-references and not the decision. A `NOTE:` at `RemoteServerConnection._lastActivityTicks` now carries it, with the counting made explicit: there are two stamp *fields*, one expression each and both in `src/engine/Server`, while the engine's third monotonic measure is a local `Stopwatch`, which is already the shared primitive for that shape. The condition has therefore not tripped, and extracting a type for two one-line expressions would be more code than it removes. A third field of that kind is the trigger.

**Checked and found correct — the things the handoff put on the agenda.**

- **The flake margin on the new case** (a ten-second threshold against a one-second ping, so ten missed pings before the sweep can act). Accepted as argued and not churned: a smaller threshold would save about 3 s of suite time and spend the margin that keeps the case from failing when one ping is late on a loaded machine.
- **`ExecuteScript("begin end;")` as the liveness probe.** An empty D4 block is compiled and run with no special case anywhere in the engine; polling it at 250 ms for up to 60 s changed nothing on the live connection, and the control was reclaimed at the expected 10 to 12 s in every run.
- **The sweep now reads a fresh clock per connection** where the old code froze one cut-off instant for the whole sweep. During a slow sweep — one connection whose process will not stop costs up to half of `Engine.ProcessTerminationTimeout`, 15 s by default — a later connection is now measured against the clock as it is when its decision is reached rather than as it was at sweep start, so it can be relinquished in the same sweep where before it survived to the next. That only ever moves a reclamation later than the threshold, never earlier, so the documented 300-to-375-second window still holds and each decision is made on the connection's true idle time. Left as it is.
- **The new case leaves its session and process open and lets `localServer.Dispose()` take them down.** That is the product's own teardown: `LocalServer.Dispose` stops the keep-alive and then relinquishes the connection, which closes its sessions. It is the first case in the fixture to run that path with a live process on it, which is coverage rather than a leak.
- **The `Volatile` pair claiming only ordering, and `_syncHandle` deliberately not taken.** Both accurate, the second for the stated reason: `BeginCall` holds that handle for the whole of `Connect` and `Disconnect`, so a sweep that took it would serialize itself against clients' in-flight calls.

**No tickets filed.** Nothing reached the filing bar. Both arms' root-cause sites were the two the implement ticket named, and both changed. The one class-level option — rung 3, a build-time guard against subtracting two wall-clock readings — was weighed again against my own survey and declined for the reason the implement ticket recorded: the legitimate wall-clock uses outnumber the defects by an order of magnitude (the D4 `Now()` and `Today()` operators, `ServerTransaction.StartTime`, every log timestamp, and about fourteen reported-duration sites in `ScriptExecutionUtility`, `ServerPlan`, `ServerProcess`, `TestNodes` and `D4Editor`), so the guard would be mostly a hand-maintained allowlist, and there is no decision-grade instance left for it to catch. The record a future reader meets instead is the three cross-referencing remarks and the plan's **Decisions** bullet. The nearest thing to a remaining instance is the `System.Diagnostics.Benchmark*` D4 operators in `SystemNodes.cs`, which return a wall-clock difference *to a D4 program* rather than printing it; a clock step makes one reported duration wrong and nothing else, changing a D4 operator's behaviour is a compatibility surface this ticket has no mandate over, and the full enumeration is archived with the implement ticket.

**No other tripwires, and no accepted-tradeoff notes touched.** Nothing in this change newly became conditional: the two conditional concerns a reader might expect at these sites were already recorded before this ticket — the serial sweep's per-stuck-process delay, as a `NOTE:` in `RelinquishIdleConnections`, and the watcher-thread extraction condition in `NativeSessionManager` — and neither moved.

**What a human still has to watch by hand.** A server running across a daylight-saving transition, or one whose clock an operator corrects by more than the idle threshold. Before this change the first sweep after a forward step relinquished every connection at once; now nothing happens. It is not reproducible in the suite and not reproducible in process at all, so if anyone has a machine whose clock they can step, that is the one observation this ticket cannot make for itself.
