## What landed

Two mechanisms that do nothing on their own and that `bug-native-cli-sessions-never-reclaimed` needs before its idle watcher can be added safely.

**A clock per native session, on the server.** `NativeSession` carries a last-activity stamp taken from `Environment.TickCount64` (stamped in its constructor, so a new session is never already idle), a count of the calls in flight on it, and `IdleSeconds`, which reports 0 while that count is above zero. `NativeServer.GetNativeSession` became `AcquireNativeSession` (look up, raise the same "Invalid session handle" error, count the call, stamp) paired with `ReleaseNativeSession`, both wholly inside `lock (_nativeSessions)`; the seven operations that take a session handle now acquire, work in a `try`, and release in a `finally`. That shape also closed a pre-existing data race - the old lookup read the dictionary with no lock while the add and remove wrote it under one, under `ConcurrencyMode.Multiple`. One public read was added for the test, `NativeServer.GetSessionIdleSeconds()`, and one public accessor, `DataphorServiceHost.NativeServer`.

**A keep-alive on the client, in `NativeCLISession`.** Shaped after `LocalServer`'s: a two-minute default, a constructor overload taking the interval in seconds (0 for none), one background thread per session signalled on `Dispose`, and the ping itself - `GetTransactionCount` on the session's own handle, already on the contract, side-effect free, and reaching the server by the same acquire path every other operation uses, so neither the contract nor the server needed a special case. Every operation of the class is now inside one lock, which the ping takes with `Monitor.TryEnter` and no wait, so a ping is skipped rather than queued while the owner is in a call.

Nothing reads the clock yet and nothing is reclaimed. What changes observably is the periodic call from a client that holds a session, and the serialization of one `NativeCLISession`'s operations against each other.

Delivered by `ticket(implement): native-cli-session-liveness-clock`, with the clock, the keep-alive, three tripwire `NOTE:`s, the end-to-end case in `tests/DAE.Server.Tests/NativeSessionKeepAliveTest.cs`, and the `docs/migration-notes.md` entry. The review pass narrowed three members' visibility, corrected two stale document statements, bounded one claim in a remark, and parked two duplication findings at their sites.

## Review findings

**Checked by reading and running.** The implement diff in full, before the handoff summary; the lock discipline on `_nativeSessions` line by line; every path that can reach `EnterCall` and `ExitCall`, for a count that could go negative or never come back down; the client's two locks for an ordering that could deadlock; the interval arithmetic for overflow; the ping's target down to the field it reads (`ServerProcess.TransactionCount` is `_transactions.Count`); `LocalServer`'s keep-alive beside the copy of it, for a correction made there and not carried over; the real caller (`RemoteSessionImplementation`) and the prior invariant on the method the new lock wraps; the three tripwires the implement pass parked; the four tickets this change's comments cite; the new test; and every document the change touches as well as the two it should have.

**Four findings, all minor, all fixed in this pass.**

- **`docs/architecture.md` was left asserting the opposite of what landed.** Its catalog write-lock paragraph said of native CLI sessions that "nothing tracks their liveness" - true before this commit and false after it. Rewritten to say what is now true: each native session carries a clock that every call moves, the shipped client pings every 120 seconds to keep it moving, and nothing reads the clock yet, so the write lock is still held until the server is stopped.
- **The same clause in `docs/modernization-plan.md`.** The WS2 paragraph "The door still open" described `NativeServer` as having "no liveness tracking, no idle sweep and no ping in `INativeCLIService`". The handoff judged the whole paragraph still true; only the sweep clause was. Rewritten to carry the clock, the in-use count and the client's ping - and why the contract still needs no `Ping` operation of its own - with the paragraph's point intact: nothing reclaims these sessions, and the fix lands in two steps.
- **`NativeSession.EnterCall`, `ExitCall` and `IdleSeconds` were `public` on a public type.** All three are only correct under `NativeServer`'s session lock, which nothing outside that assembly can take, and a caller that counted a call in and not out would leave a session looking busy for ever while one that counted out twice would let the watcher reclaim a busy session. Made `internal` - their only callers are in `NativeServer`, and the test reads `GetSessionIdleSeconds()` instead - with the reason recorded in the region comment. This also keeps three members out of the public surface `docs/migration-notes.md` has to describe; its entry never listed them.
- **`NativeCLISession.Dispose`'s remark claimed more than the code now does.** It opens "The local half is released whatever the network does", which `engine-server-link-session-outlives-drop` established, and the new lock means the stop first waits for a ping in flight. Bounded in the remark rather than changed in the code: that wait is unbounded on a dead-but-open socket because the binding sets no send or receive time-out (`DataphorServiceUtility`, both `TimeSpan.MaxValue`), but so is the stop itself on the same channel, which is what the method already waited on - so the release is delayed by at most one more round trip than before, and a caller that can afford neither needs a bounded time-out on the binding. `ServerLinkConnectionTest.TestStoppingAProcessWhoseOtherServerHasGoneDoesNotThrow`, which pins that invariant, passes.

**Two tripwires recorded, both about duplication the implement pass introduced deliberately.** Parked at their sites rather than filed, because each is conditional on a growth that has not happened.

- `src/engine/Server/NativeCLI/NativeServer.cs`, at `AcquireNativeSession`: the acquire/`try`/`finally` is written out at seven call sites, and an eighth written without the `finally` would leave its session counted in for ever - which, once the sweep lands, is a session nothing can reclaim. Left unwrapped because there is no delegate or guard idiom anywhere else in this assembly to wrap it with (two `Func`/`Action` uses in all of `src/engine/DAE`, none in `src/engine/Server`), so a helper would be a new local idiom in the file the next ticket opens; and because `INativeCLIService`'s shape is a compatibility surface this project's rules say cannot change without a ticket that says so, an eighth operation cannot arrive unreviewed. The note says to introduce the guard if the set ever grows.
- `src/protocol/NativeCLI/NativeCLISession.cs`, at the *Keep Alive* region: the constants, the fields and the three methods are `LocalServer`'s, copied rather than shared, though both assemblies reference `src/protocol/Common` and a shared primitive could live there. Agreed with as written - it is the same call `bug-native-cli-sessions-never-reclaimed` records for the two idle watchers, and extracting one now would rework a path `port-abandoned-session-cleanup` has only just proven, for no behaviour. The note says to extract on the third, and that a shared one is also where the one-thread-per-session change already noted there would belong.

**Verified and nothing found.** Each of these was a candidate defect read through to an answer, and none needed a test to settle.

- The in-use count cannot go negative or be stranded: `AcquireNativeSession` sits outside the `try` at all seven sites, and its invalid-handle throw happens before `EnterCall`, so a release runs exactly once per successful acquire.
- Every access to `_nativeSessions` is the declaration, a `lock` statement, or a line inside such a block - twelve lines, re-checked after this pass's edits.
- The client's locks cannot deadlock: `Dispose` releases the keep-alive reference lock inside `StopKeepAlive` before it takes the operation lock, the ping takes only the operation lock, and the keep-alive thread's `finally` takes only the reference lock and never while holding the other.
- The interval arithmetic cannot overflow: the ceiling is `Int32.MaxValue / 1000`, so the millisecond wait tops out at 2,147,483,000. The 0 case starts no thread and leaves the signal null, which `StopKeepAlive` tolerates, so disposing a session with no keep-alive is safe.
- The ping has no side effect and takes no lock on the far server: `GetTransactionCount` reads the length of the session process's transaction list. It cannot contend with that session's own work either, because the client skips a ping while its owner is in a call, which is exactly when the server counts the session in use.
- A second `Dispose` is still a no-op, and the batch overload still acquires once for the whole batch, so a long batch keeps its session in use throughout rather than between operations.
- `LocalServer.StartKeepAlive` has a "started more than once" guard the copy omits; harmless here, since the only caller is the constructor and the 0 case returns before the signal is created.
- The one new concurrent caller the client gained does not add a site to `bug-wcf-client-channel-field-is-unsynchronized`: the ping goes through the same lock as every other operation, so `ServiceClient<T>`'s lazy channel field is still reached from one thread at a time through this class. Nothing to append to that ticket, and the same for the other three the comments cite - each is already named at the right site.

**Tests: the one new case kept, nothing cut, nothing added.**

- `TheSessionsClockMovesWhileTheClientHoldsIt` is the only thing that shows the deliverable end to end - that the client really pings and that the server's clock really moves back - and it does so at the lowest layer that can show it, since the ping belongs to the client library and crosses the wire. Re-run in this pass: aged to 2 s after 2.0 s, back to 0 s 1.1 s later, 7 s for the case. Its three-second interval is right for the reason the fixture gives: idle time is reported in whole seconds.
- No case added for the new constructor's bounds (0, negative, above 2,147,483). `LocalServer`'s identical check, which this one was copied from, has none either, and the handoff's own judgement - three lines the compiler does not cover but that no behaviour depends on - is right.
- No case added for the ping-skip, failed-ping or `Dispose`-ordering paths. The handoff is right that the sibling fixture's trick does not transfer: `ConnectionKeepAliveTest` wraps `IRemoteServer` in a decorator, and here the ping goes through a WCF client rather than an interface a test can wrap. The `Dispose`-while-the-keep-alive-is-running path is in fact walked by the new case, which disposes a session with a live three-second ping; what is unasserted is only that nothing escapes, which NUnit asserts for us.

**No tickets filed and nothing routed to `blocked/`.** Deliberately, not for want of looking: the four findings were document and visibility corrections fixable in the pass, and the two remaining are conditional on growth that has not happened, which the review rules put in code as tripwires rather than on the board. **One claim a reader should not take further than it goes.** The data race the acquire/release shape closed is verified by inspection only. That is the right standard for it - a data race has no deterministic reproduction - but it means the fix rests on the twelve-line lock audit above and on nothing else.
