## What landed

Two WCF clients kept their channel in a field and rebuilt it when it was unusable, with no guard — while two threads really reach that field, because the shipped client pings its connection from a keep-alive thread of its own while the application calls through the same object. The same eleven lines, written twice: `ServiceClient<T>.GetInterface` (the base of `ListenerClient` and `NativeCLIClient`) and `ClientServer.GetServiceInterface`. Both now go through one guarded holder, so the pattern exists once and the bad states are unreachable rather than merely unlikely.

**`src/protocol/Common/WCF/ChannelHolder.cs`** — new public class `Alphora.Common.WCF.ChannelHolder<T>` (`ChannelHolder(Func<T> createChannel)`, `T GetChannel()`, `Dispose()`). `GetChannel` holds one private lock around the whole decision — test the held channel's state, reuse it or create a replacement — and returns the local the call settled on rather than a re-read of the field. A channel being replaced is aborted, not closed, and aborted after the lock is released.

Three shape decisions, each of which was one of the defects:

- **Usable means "not `Faulted`, `Closing` or `Closed`"**, so `Created` and `Opening` count as usable. A channel out of `ChannelFactory<T>.CreateChannel()` is in state `Created` and only reaches `Opened` once the first call on it has connected, so the old `State == Opened` test meant every caller created a new channel for the duration of a whole network round trip, and all but one were discarded. The cache the class exists to provide did not hit at all over exactly the interval the two threads compete over.
- **The replaced channel is aborted, not dropped.** `Abort()` and not `Close()`, because the channel being replaced is faulted, superseded or closing, and a `Close()` against a server that has gone waits without limit (`DataphorServiceUtility` sets both binding time-outs to `TimeSpan.MaxValue`). Only `Dispose` closes, and only when the channel reports `Opened`.
- **No fault handler at all.** Reading the state under the lock on every call is what the `Faulted` handler was doing; deleting it removes the third writer of the field, and with it the question of a fault raised on a communication-stack thread.

**`ServiceClient<T>`** gained `where T : class` and lost `_channel`, `SetChannel`, `ChannelFaulted`, `IsChannelFaulted` (already unused), `IsChannelValid` and `CloseChannel`; `GetInterface()` is one line over a `readonly` holder built in the constructor. **`ClientServer`** kept its own `ChannelFactory<IClientDataphorService>` (deliberately not moved onto the shared `ChannelFactoryCache` — that changes factory lifetime and when `DataphorServiceUtility.ServiceEndpointHook` runs, and is a separate question), lost the same six members, and builds its holder in `Open()` over the factory *local* rather than the field. `GetServiceInterface()` reads the holder field once into a local and raises `ServerException.Codes.ServerInactive` when it is absent, instead of passing `CheckActive()` (now deleted — that was its only caller) and then dereferencing a field `Close()` had nulled.

`ReportCommunicationError`'s commented-out body became a `// NOTE: accepted tradeoff — …` comment stating what was declined (closing the channel on a communication failure), why (unwinding against a vanished server took ten times as long), the revisit condition (a bounded time-out on the client binding), and what the holder's state test now covers instead.

**Tests.** `tests/Common.Tests/ChannelHolderTest.cs` pins the holder with no WCF and no network in it — the holder reaches a channel only through `ICommunicationObject`, so a stub with a settable state, abort and close counters, and a hook that runs inside the state read shows every case. Five cases: a channel is reused from the moment it is created (defect c); a faulted channel is replaced and the replaced one aborted exactly once and never closed (defect b's shutdown half); a fault raised from inside the holder's own state read does not make `GetChannel` return null (defect a, deterministic and single-threaded — the arm that previously needed a real server and a severed connection); disposal closes the held channel and refuses later calls by `ObjectDisposedException`; and, under four threads × 2,500 calls with stubs that fault themselves every few calls, every channel created was handed to a caller, every channel replaced was aborted exactly once, none was closed, and the number aborted is the number created less the one still held. `CoreWcfProtocolTest.TestACallOnAClosedClientServerIsRefusedByCode` pins the client layer: a closed `ClientServer` refuses `GetServiceInterface()` with `ServerInactive`, not `NullReferenceException`; it needs no server, because constructing with an override port contacts nothing.

**Documents.** `docs/modernization-plan.md` carries K38, covering all three arms, both sites, the holder's shape, the measurements and the test that pins each. `docs/migration-notes.md` carries four rows under *Custom C# extensions*: the new public `ChannelHolder<T>`; the `where T : class` constraint on `ServiceClient<T>` together with the post-disposal exception change; the `ClientServer.Open()` failure behaviour (below); and the deployment-visible change, which is fewer sockets held per client. `docs/architecture.md`'s assembly inventory names the holder as part of `AlphoraDataphorCommon`.

Nothing here touches a contract shape, an operation signature, a port, an error code, a registered class name or an on-disk format.

## Review findings

**What was checked.** The implement diff (`ticket(implement): bug-wcf-client-channel-field-is-unsynchronized`) was read before the handoff summary. Every claim the K38 row and the new comments make about the *old* code was checked line by line against the pre-fix source, and each holds: the three reads of the field on the fast path, the overwrite without a close, and the `Opened`-only validity test. The tree was grepped for other sites of the pattern (`CreateChannel()` across `src/`, `libraries/` and `legacy/`): exactly the two holder lambdas, so no third site was missed. It was grepped for every deleted member (`IsChannelFaulted`, `IsChannelValid`, `CloseChannel`, `SetChannel`, `ChannelFaulted`, `ClientServer.CheckActive`): no caller anywhere, the remaining `CheckActive` hits all being other classes' own. `ChannelFactoryCache` — the one adjacent piece of shared mutable state in the same assembly — was read in full and is correctly locked, including the release path the wrapper's `Disposed` event runs, so the class of defect is retired across the assembly rather than at two sites of it.

Three things that had to be true for the fix to be safe, each confirmed rather than assumed. **No network I/O under the lock:** `ChannelFactory<T>.CreateChannel()` builds the channel stack and opens the factory; the connect happens on the first operation, which is also why `Created` must count as usable. **No lock-order inversion:** `NativeCLISession` takes its session lock outside the holder's, nothing takes them the other way round, and `ChannelFactoryCache`'s lock is taken in the `ServiceClient` constructor and never inside the holder's lambda. **No channel can be created on a released factory:** at both sites the holder is disposed before the thing that makes its channels is released, and a thread creating a channel at that instant holds the holder's lock, so the release cannot overtake it and the `_disposed` flag refuses creation afterwards. `GetChannel`'s exception safety was checked too — `_channel` is assigned after `_createChannel()` returns, so a throwing factory leaves the held channel in place and does not abort it. The claim that `SendKeepAlive` swallows the `ObjectDisposedException` a dispose/ping race now raises was verified at `LocalServer.SendKeepAlive`, which catches everything.

Docs: the WS5 sentence at `docs/modernization-plan.md` line 280 and the keep-alive paragraph at `docs/architecture.md` line 71 were read against what landed and are correct — line 280 is the sentence that was *not* true before this fix. `python tools/docs-link-check/check_links.py docs --pending tools/docs-convert/legacy-links.tsv` reports 0 errors. Hygiene: every file touched keeps its tabs, CRLF endings and byte-order-mark state; `ChannelHolder.cs` is 172 lines, `ChannelHolderTest.cs` 311, `ServiceClient.cs` 133, `ClientServer.cs` 468 (`wc -l`), and `CoreWcfProtocolTest.cs`'s size-debt `NOTE:` reads 767 lines, which is what it measures.

**The implementer's tests, reviewed as code.** Nothing was cut. All five assert exact counters and name what a failure means, so none can pass vacuously; the threaded case creates about 3,300 channels, so its "a collision actually happened" guard is not a flake. The stub is a test double for a framework interface, not for a module this repository owns, so the cases sit at the right layer. One was added, for a contract nothing verified — see below.

**Fixed in this pass (minor).**

- **`ServiceClient<T>.GetInterface()` after `Dispose()` raised `NullReferenceException`** — the gap the handoff declared. `_channelHolder` is now `readonly` and `Dispose` no longer nulls it; the holder's own `Dispose` is idempotent, so the call is refused by `ObjectDisposedException` naming the object. Made `readonly` rather than merely leaving the assignment out, so that "the holder is never null" is a fact the compiler keeps and `GetInterface` needs no guard. The migration-notes row for `ServiceClient<T>` carries the exception change.
- **`ClientServer.Open()` left the client half open if it threw after the factory was built.** `IsActive` is `_channelFactory != null`, and the factory field was assigned in the middle of the method while the holder was built at the end — so a `ServiceEndpointHook` supplied by a host application that threw in between left an object reporting `IsActive` true that could never answer anything but `ServerInactive`, for its whole life, with `Open()` declining to retry. (Before this change the same throw left a client that still worked, so it was a narrow regression, not merely a latent edge.) Both are now settled in locals and the factory field is assigned last, so `IsActive` is true only once there is a holder to answer with and a failed `Open()` can simply be called again. `Close()` lost its outer `if (IsActive)`, each field being tested in its own right, so it also tidies up a client left half open; closing one that was never opened still does nothing. A migration-notes row records it.
- **A comment that a reader could have disproved.** `ChannelHolder.Abort`'s remark justified abort-over-close by saying the client's binding "does not set" a send time-out. It does set it — to `TimeSpan.MaxValue` in `DataphorServiceUtility` — which is the same conclusion by a different route, but the comment as written was false against the code. Reworded to name the value, at both places in the class that rely on it.
- **`ChannelHolder.Close` had no remark on the one thing worth knowing about it:** it is the only place the class waits on the network, and with no bounded time-out that wait has no limit against a server that has gone. Added, with why disposal is the one caller for which an orderly shutdown is worth attempting.
- The exception-safety comment inside `GetChannel` sat above `abandoned = _channel;` while describing the line below it; moved onto the line it describes. `docs/architecture.md`'s one-line inventory of `AlphoraDataphorCommon` did not mention the new public type; it does now.

**Test added (one).** `ChannelHolderTest.TestDisposeClosesTheHeldChannelAndRefusesLaterCalls`. Disposal was the one part of the holder's documented contract that nothing verified, and the `Opened → Close`, otherwise `Abort` branch inside it had no coverage at all; it is also the behaviour the first finding above changed. The case asserts that an open held channel is closed exactly once and not aborted, that a call afterwards is refused by `ObjectDisposedException`, and that a second `Dispose` does not shut the channel down again. `tests/Common.Tests` now runs 18 cases in about a second.

**Major findings: none.** Not "looks good" — the reason is that the ticket's root cause was retired by representation rather than patched: after this change the only mutable channel state in the protocol assemblies is inside the holder, every path to it goes through one lock, and the two surviving fields (`ClientServer._channelHolder`, `ServiceClient._channelHolder`) are each written once per open and read into a local before use. The two things a major finding could have been — a third unconverted site, and an unlocked sibling cache — were both looked for specifically and are not there.

**Recorded as a tripwire, not a ticket: none, deliberately.** Two conditional concerns were considered and both resolved to something better than a comment. The `Open()` inconsistency above was fixed instead of noted, because the fix was smaller than the note and removes the state rather than describing it. The unbounded `Close()` on disposal is already recorded as an accepted tradeoff at `ClientServer.ReportCommunicationError`, with its revisit condition (a bounded time-out on the client binding); the behaviour is unchanged from before this fix, so by the accepted-tradeoff rule it is not re-opened — the remark added to `ChannelHolder.Close` points the next reader at it from the site where the wait actually happens.

**Checked and left alone — the rest of what the handoff put on the agenda.**

- **Defect (a) is pinned at the holder, not end to end.** Agreed and unchanged: forcing the original `NullReferenceException` through a real client needs a channel already `Opened` that faults between two reads of a field that no longer exists. The holder's test raises the fault from inside the state read, which is that instant, deterministically.
- **Concurrent calls now share one channel earlier than before.** Two threads on a freshly built client used to get two channels because neither was `Opened` yet, and now get one. This is safe for the reason the change depends on: WCF serializes the implicit open inside the channel, so a second caller on a channel in `Opening` waits for that open rather than racing it, and once a channel had opened the old code shared it too. The listener, native-CLI and out-of-process protocol fixtures all pass.
- **The throwaway console program that measured 8,000 channels is gone and nothing re-measures the 44–123 ns lock against the 13–24 µs channel creation.** Left as it is: the numbers are recorded in `ChannelHolder`'s remarks and in the K38 row, the margin is five orders of magnitude, and the standing check of the accounting is the threaded holder test, which is cheap enough to keep.
- **The `NativeCLISession` remark and the re-measured size `NOTE:` in `CoreWcfProtocolTest`**, both edits beyond the implement TODO, were read and are accurate.
