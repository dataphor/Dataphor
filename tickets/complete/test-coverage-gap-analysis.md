## What landed

The whole suite ran once under Microsoft's coverage collector, the measured report is published at [docs/coverage-report.md](../../docs/coverage-report.md), and eight tickets came out of it - six from the implementation, two more from the review. No engine code changed at any point; the only code-adjacent change is three file paths added to the report tool's data file.

- **`docs/coverage-report.md`** (new) - a hand-written head (run provenance, what the numbers mean and do not, how to reproduce, what instrumentation cost, what the pass found, the tickets filed) followed by the tool's generated tables, which the review verified are byte-identical to a regeneration from the retained collection.
- **`docs/README.md`** - one line in *Project documents*.
- **`tools/coverage-report/areas.json`** - one new `excluded` entry covering three compiled files that nothing references.
- **`tools/coverage-report/README.md`** and the `NOTE:` at the top of **`coverage_report.py`** - both promised to report the whole-suite cost of instrumentation once someone measured it. They now report it, and the per-script-coverage question is parked in the README as a `NOTE:` tripwire.
- **`tickets/backlog/debt-ws12-hygiene-remainder.md`** - one new arm, `hygiene-remove-dead-compiled-files`, now naming four files.

## The measurement

Build clean (0 errors, 143 warnings, all pre-existing). Three parts, each its own foreground command, all with collection: **582 passed, 0 failed, 24 skipped** - 21 scripts the manifest records as known failures and 3 it records as fragments, with no new failure of any kind. Nothing went to `tickets/.pre-existing-error.md` and no entry in `tickets/.pre-existing-known.md` was triggered.

Engine total **66.0 %** (67,020 of 101,513 lines); `Alphora.Dataphor.DAE` 68.9 %, `AlphoraDataphorServer` 53.6 %, `Alphora.Dataphor` 36.2 %. The libraries and client stack roll up to 17.9 %, the two large holes being `Alphora.Dataphor.DAE.Device.SQL` at 0.2 % of 6,143 lines and `Alphora.Dataphor.Frontend.Server` at 14.2 % of 5,157. The report carries every figure and the per-area table.

Instrumentation turned out to be cheap and quiet, which was the open question: **+16 % on the longest part** (6 m 03 s with collection against a 5 m 13 s control, part 1 only), no case that failed only under collection, and the suite's one wall-clock-sensitive case - `AbandonedClientProcessTest` - moved from 5.1 s to 5.4 s against a 30 s limit. The measurement also showed that the part timings in the root `AGENTS.md` are stale independently of collection; the report says so, and refreshing them is another ticket's business.

## The tickets

Eight. Six were filed by the implementation: `debt-realsql-coverage` (RealSQL, 0.0 % of 1,423 lines), `debt-sql-translation-coverage` (the engine's SQL emitter, 0.0 % of 1,031), `debt-library-upgrades-coverage` (49.9 % of 1,907), `debt-streams-coverage` (40.7 % of 1,129), `debt-debugger-coverage` (35.7 % of 894) and `debt-tableindexer-coverage` (five error codes at zero hits inside an otherwise well-covered region). Only two areas met the tool's automatic gap bar, so the eight-ticket cap never bound.

The review filed two more from the per-file data: `debt-coverage-report-zero-files` and `debt-unreachable-redefine-nodes`, both described under *Review findings* below.

## Review findings

The review read the implement diff (`ticket(implement): test-coverage-gap-analysis`) first, then verified every published number against the Cobertura collection the run left in `artifacts/coverage/` - which made this an unusually checkable pass, because nothing had to be taken on trust.

**Checked and correct.** The generated half of `docs/coverage-report.md` regenerates byte-identically from the retained collection, exit code 0, 21 areas, 2 gaps, no unassigned file and no unlisted assembly. Every per-file figure quoted in all six tickets matches the collection. The two counterfactual rates the report publishes for the dead-code exclusion - Streams 35.7 % and Base utilities 38.4 % with the three files counted - reproduce exactly when the exclusion is removed. The three excluded files are referenced by nothing outside their own `Compile` entries, confirmed by the greps the handoff names. The table-indexer region of `Compiler.cs` is 86.9 %, 86 of 99 lines, with lines 12,614 / 12,625 / 12,684 / 12,709 / 12,713 at zero hits and the ambiguity block at 12,677-12,685 too - and all five error codes (105259, 105260, 105261, 105264, 105275) check out against `CompilerException.cs`. The claims that carry the tickets' cases also hold: `SetLanguage(String)` is granted to role `User`, Dataphoria switches to RealSQL when a document's designer is `SQL`, `Binary` and `Graphic` and their operators are granted to `User`, `FileStreamProvider` is reachable only under a `#define` that is commented out, `AbandonedClientProcessTest` really does allow 30 s against a 5 s threshold, and the coverage manifest is untouched.

**The judgement call the handoff flagged - excluding three dead files from the area rollup - is right, and for a reason the report did not give.** The precedent it followed (the REST exclusion: compiled, uncalled, excluded, hygiene arm named) is the governing one, and the exclusion is also *forward-consistent*: when the hygiene arm deletes the files they stop being coverable, so today's 40.7 % is tomorrow's 40.7 %. Counting them would have published a rate that silently improves on deletion without anything actually getting better. No change made.

**Major - two tickets filed.**

- `debt-coverage-report-zero-files`. The report tool prints a per-file breakdown only for areas it classifies as `gap`, so a file at 0 % inside a well-covered area appears nowhere: not in the area table, which shows an average, and not in a per-file table, because the area is not a gap. On this run that hid **21 files at 0 %, 1,300 coverable lines**, eleven of them 20 lines or more. This is the general fix rather than a ticket per file, because the same blind spot produced three separate findings below - the per-file data is already in the analysis and already in `--json`; only the rendering withholds it. The report now carries the list by hand, under *Wholly untested files the area rates hide*, and that section goes when the tool prints it.
- `debt-unreachable-redefine-nodes`. The largest of those hidden files is not untested, it is unreachable. `RedefineNode.cs` (257 lines), `RedefineTable.cs` (65) and the class `RowRedefineNode` (124) are at exactly zero hits while the D4 `redefine` operator is executed by four passing scripts, because `EmitRedefineNode` in `Compiler.cs` puts the node-emitting branch behind `#if USEREDEFINENODE` - a symbol defined nowhere in the repository - and the live branch rewrites `redefine` into add/remove/rename. The disabled branch is stale as well as off: it still uses the `A`-prefixed identifiers the repository renamed away, so it would not compile if the symbol were defined. These are not free to delete, which is why they are their own ticket and not another hygiene arm: `System.RedefineNode` and `System.RowRedefineNode` are the registered D4 classes of `iRedefine(row)` and `iRedefine(table)` in `SystemCatalog.d4`, and `SQLDeviceMappings.d4` maps `iRedefine` in eight places - a compatibility surface, so the ticket asks a human to retire or deliberately keep rather than assuming either.

**Minor - fixed in this pass.** Five, all of them inaccuracies in text a later reader would have acted on.

- **The reproduce command in `docs/coverage-report.md` destroyed the document it was in.** The tool writes a whole document of its own, head included, so the published `-o docs/coverage-report.md` would have replaced sixty lines of hand-written provenance, meaning, evidence and ticket index with the tool's three-line preamble - silently, on the first person who followed the instructions. The command now writes beside the collection, with a bolded line saying why and what to carry over by hand.
- **The Base utilities rationale was incomplete in the direction that mattered.** It said the area's holes "are not engine behaviour" and attributed them to the Frontend, naming `BOP/Inheritance.cs`, `BOP/Dilx.cs` and `CRC32.cs`. It omitted `MemoryUtility.cs`, 161 lines at 0 % and the third-largest zero in the area, which the D4 operators `Diagnostics.GetInstanceSize` and `Diagnostics.GetInstanceSizes` call. Corrected, and the reason for leaving it unticketed is now stated as what it is - a diagnostic rather than a data path - instead of resting on an argument that does not cover it.
- **A fourth dead compiled file was missed, in the area whose rationale described its absence wrongly.** `src/engine/Server/Connection/GenericDotNetConnection.cs` (111 lines, 0 %) is a compiled public ADO.NET provider-factory wrapper that nothing in the repository references, and the report described *Device connections (engine)* as having 0 % files that "are all the MSSQL connection and command classes". Corrected, and the file is now named on the `debt-ws12-hygiene-remainder` arm - with the caveat that it should be weighed separately from the other three, since a generic provider-factory connection is something a customer's custom device plausibly could have built on. It was deliberately **not** added to the coverage exclusion: the implementation's own precedent for a file whose fate is unsettled is `FileStreamProvider`, left counted and documented, and that is the better treatment for one a human may decide to keep.
- **`debt-debugger-coverage` credited the suite with coverage it does not have.** It said `DebugLibrary.d4` sets break-on-start and attaches and detaches a session, checking each against `Debug.GetSessions()`. The measurement says `SetBreakOnStart`, `GetSessions()` and `DetachSession` are all at **0 %**; the script's session attach/detach pair is commented out with its author's note that it needs a second session, and its one `AttachSession` call asserts a *refusal*, which is why that node reads 50 %. The same list wrongly put `Debug.Stop()` in the untested half, where it is at 100 %. Both passages rewritten against the per-class numbers. This mattered because that section is what tells an implementer not to re-cover ground.
- **`debt-library-upgrades-coverage` made the opposite error.** It listed nine life-cycle operators and said "each of those is a separate uncovered entry point today"; five of them (`RegisterLibrary`, `EnsureLibraryRegistered`, `UnregisterLibrary`, `DropLibrary`, `GetLibraryDescriptor`) execute at 100 % and two more partly. Rewritten to name the operators actually at zero - the attach, refresh and especially the **upgrade** set, about 263 lines of which not one line has ever run - so the ticket's weight now sits where the measurement puts it.

**Conditional or speculative - none recorded.** Nothing found here had the "fine now, matters if X later" shape: the findings were either wrong text, which is fixed, or measured absences, which are ticketed. The pass's own tripwire, the per-script-coverage question parked in the tool README, was reviewed and left as it stands.

**Considered and declined - none met.** No accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:` sits at any site this review touched. The one `NOTE:` in range, `StreamUtility.cs` line 122 on the CA2022 partial-read assumption, is a tripwire rather than a decline, and `debt-streams-coverage` already tells its reader not to re-open it.

**Tests - none added, and none owed.** The project rule attaches a characterization test to work that ports, re-hosts or changes a subsystem's behaviour, or reproduces a bug. Neither the implementation nor this review did any of those: the implementation ran an existing command, published a document and filed tickets, and the review corrected prose and filed two tickets. The only behaviour-bearing change in the whole ticket is the report tool's exclusion handling, which the tool's own suite already covers. The implementation's tests were reviewed as code like the rest: there are none to cut, because it wrote none, correctly.

**Not re-examined, and still open.** The `+16 %` figure is a part-1 measurement against a part-1 control; parts 2 and 3 were not re-run without collection, by the implementation or by the review, and the report says so. Wiring the collector into CI remains a decision nobody has taken. The nine remaining hidden zero-coverage files were not investigated beyond their line counts, and `debt-coverage-report-zero-files` says as much rather than guessing at them.

## For whoever picks a ticket up next

- **Do not re-run the collection just to read a number.** It costs about 14 minutes over three commands. The rates are published, and `artifacts/coverage/` holds the run's Cobertura files until somebody cleans `artifacts/` - the per-file and per-class detail behind every figure in the report is answerable from them with a short script.
- **A ticket's area figure is from this run.** If a figure in a ticket and a figure in the report disagree, the report is right and the ticket has a copy error; the review checked all of them once, but a later edit could introduce one.
- **The report's head is hand-written and the tables are not.** Regenerate into a scratch file and carry the tables over; the document now says so in its *Reproducing it* section.
