## What changed

The server reclaims a client's work by watching one clock per **connection** (`RemoteServerConnection.LastActivityTime`), and nothing moves that clock except the client calling `Ping()`. The client was pinging from `LocalSession`, one thread per **session**. The two did not line up. The keep-alive now belongs to `LocalServer`, which is the object that owns the connection.

**`src/protocol/Client/Server/LocalServer.cs`**

- A new `#region Keep Alive` at the bottom of the class, holding `DefaultKeepAliveInterval` (120, the old `LocalSession.LocalKeepAliveInterval` under a new name), the `_keepAliveInterval` field, and `StartKeepAlive`/`KeepAlive`/`StopKeepAlive` moved across from `LocalSession`. The strategy comment moved with them and was corrected: the 120 seconds is the client's interval, the five minutes is the server's threshold (`ConnectionManager.IdleTimeInSeconds`), and the only relation between them is that the server's has to be the larger.
- The thread is now a **background** thread. It was a foreground thread, so an undisposed client kept the process alive after `Main` returned; the server's own idle watcher was made a background thread for the same reason and the comment points at it.
- `StartKeepAlive()` is the last statement of the constructor, after `Establish`; `StopKeepAlive()` is the first thing `Dispose` does, before the relinquish.
- New public constructor overload `LocalServer(IRemoteServer, bool, string, int keepAliveIntervalSeconds)`; the existing three-argument constructor delegates to it with the default. An interval below one second is refused with `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`.
- `Dispose` now catches and ignores around **only** the `_server.Relinquish(_serverConnection)` call, so a client whose connection the server has already reclaimed can still disconnect. The comment at the site says why releasing something already released is benign.

**`src/protocol/Client/Server/LocalSession.cs`** lost `StartKeepAlive`, `KeepAlive`, `StopKeepAlive`, `_keepAliveSignal`, `_keepAliveReferenceLock`, `LocalKeepAliveInterval` and the two calls; a two-line comment points at `LocalServer`. Its `Dispose` loses one level of nesting as a result.

**`docs/migration-notes.md`** gained one row under *Custom C# extensions* covering all four visible changes: the ping is per connection, the constant moved to `LocalServer.DefaultKeepAliveInterval`, the new overload, and the background thread.

**Test-side refactor.** `WithTheObjectIDCounterRestored` moved from `CoreWcfProtocolTest` to `TestEnvironment` (public static) so both fixtures call one copy; `CoreWcfProtocolTest`'s three uses of it now call `TestEnvironment.WithTheObjectIDCounterRestored`, including the one that was written out inline in `ProtocolClient`'s constructor. No behaviour change there.

## The test

`tests/DAE.Server.Tests/ConnectionKeepAliveTest.cs`, one new fixture, at the lowest layer that shows the behaviour: a `LocalServer` over an in-process `RemoteServer` over a `TestServerInstance`. No WCF host, no port, no network. Not marked `[Parallelizable]`, like `CoreWcfProtocolTest`, because `LocalServer` starts an engine of its own. The client is built with a one-second keep-alive interval through the new overload, and the object-id counter is handed back around that construction.

| Test | What it verifies |
|---|---|
| `TheConnectionIsPingedWithNoSessionOpen` | The server's `LastActivityTime` advances on a connection where **no session was ever opened**, and advances again after a session has been opened and disconnected. These are the two windows a program holding a `LocalServer` across idle time sits in. |
| `DisposingAfterTheServerHasReclaimedTheConnectionDoesNotThrow` | `LocalServer.Dispose` completes after the connection has been relinquished behind its back, which is the state the mechanism itself produces when the pings stop. No timing involved. |

**Observed before and after, as the ticket asked.** With `StartKeepAlive()` commented out and the `catch` around the relinquish neutralised (a scratch toggle, reverted; no `git` operation was used), both cases **failed**: the first with `LastActivityTime` unmoved after the full 20-second wait, the second with the exception out of the second relinquish. With the change in place both **pass**, in about 6 s and 5 s.

## What the reviewer should look at

Honest list of what is **not** pinned by a test and where judgement was exercised.

- **Verified by inspection only**, as the ticket allowed: that many sessions on one connection now share one ping (the keep-alive starts once, in the constructor, and nothing about it depends on sessions); that the thread is a background thread; that reference-counted sharing through `ServerFactory` is unaffected, since `RemoveReference` drives `Dispose` at the last release.
- **`StopKeepAlive` signals, it does not join.** A ping already past its `WaitOne` can still land while `Dispose` is relinquishing. That is harmless - the loop's `catch` swallows whatever comes back, and the relinquish itself is now guarded - and the comment at the site says so rather than claiming a guarantee the code does not give. If the reviewer wants a join, it is a behaviour change worth arguing for separately: joining makes `Dispose` wait up to one interval on a client whose channel is wedged.
- **A ping that throws ends the keep-alive thread before the loop's cleanup block runs**, so that `ManualResetEvent` is never explicitly disposed and is reclaimed by its finalizer instead. This is the 3.1 behaviour, moved across unchanged; the ticket said to keep the failure handling as it was. Whether it is worth a `finally` is a judgement call I left to the review.
- **A second `LocalServer.Dispose`** (possible through the reference counting, and through `Dispose` being public alongside the protected override) is a no-op for the keep-alive: `StopKeepAlive` either sets an already-set signal or finds a null one. Not exercised by a test.
- **`LocalSession.cs` still has `using System.Threading;`**, now unused. Two other usings in that file were already unused before this change, so removing one and not the others seemed like arbitrary churn; say if you would rather have them all cleaned.
- **The new fixture builds a `LocalServer` against a `RemoteServer` directly**, not through `ClientServer` and the wire. That is deliberate - the behaviour is in `LocalServer` and the connection contract, and `CoreWcfProtocolTest` already covers the wired path - but it does mean the in-process failure mode of a double relinquish is a `NullReferenceException` out of `RemoteServerConnection.Dispose`, where over the wire it is the service's unknown-object-handle fault. The `catch` in `LocalServer.Dispose` is untyped and covers both; a typed catch could not.
- **The server's half is untouched**, as the ticket required: `ConnectionManager.IdleTimeInSeconds` is still 300 and `port-abandoned-session-cleanup` owns what happens when the pings stop.
