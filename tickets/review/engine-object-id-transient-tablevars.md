## What landed

The third of four steps towards `bug-object-id-counter-is-process-wide`. After this commit **the only remaining users of the process-wide counter in the whole tree are the two save-and-restore workarounds in `tests/DAE.Server.Tests/TestEnvironment.cs` and `tests/Dataphor.Benchmarks/BenchmarkServerInstance.cs`** (verified: a grep for `Object.GetNextObjectID()`, `Object.SetNextObjectID(` and `Object.GetObjectID(` over `src libraries tests tools samples` finds those two plus one commented-out line). The reason the chain shared one generator instance between every engine - keeping converted and unconverted allocation sites on one counter - is therefore spent, which is what `bug-object-id-counter-is-process-wide` needs in order to flip `Engine` to a generator of its own.

Two changes, as the ticket set out.

**The result table variable of every relational operator.** `Schema.ResultTableVar`'s constructor is now `(Plan plan, TableNode node)` and allocates from the plan; the one-argument form is gone, so the build was the census. 59 live construction sites were converted (62 textual matches, three of them inside block comments that no compiler sees: `src/engine/DAE/Debug/DebugNodes.cs:468` and `src/engine/Server/Runtime.Instructions/LibraryNodes.cs:1310` and `:1384`, left alone as the previous ticket left its commented site alone). Every one had a plan in scope except two, which the ticket predicted: `Runtime.Data/TableValue.cs` passes `Program.Plan`, and `Runtime.Instructions/TableNodes.cs` builds one in the `TableSelectorNode` **constructor**, which the ticket's census missed - that constructor now takes the plan as its first argument too, and its three callers (`Compiler.cs`, `AggregateNode.cs`, `TableNode.ExecuteDeviceInsert`) all had one in hand. A result table variable keeps a real allocation rather than `UnassignedID`, because its identifier uniquifies derived reference names.

**Objects built from a name alone.** `Schema.Object(string name)` now sets `Schema.Object.UnassignedID` instead of allocating, and is kept. The rule applied at each site is the ticket's test - *can this object reach `CacheCatalogObject`, the catalog store, or `Schema.Catalog.Add`* - not a guess from the kind of object. 52 sites now allocate, 10 pass `Schema.Object.UnassignedID` explicitly, and five types that are never numbered say so in their own remarks rather than repeating the reason at each of their 16 call sites: `Schema.SessionObject`, both `OperatorMap` classes, `Schema.RegisteredClass` and the application transaction's `TableMap`.

New identifier-taking constructors and methods, each next to the form it mirrors, all additive: `BaseTableVar(int, string, ITableType)`, `BaseTableVar(int, string, ITableType, Device)`, `BaseTableVar(int, ITableType)`, `BaseTableVar(int, ITableType, Device)`, `Library(int, string)`, `LoadedLibrary(int, string)`, `TableVarColumn.Copy(int)` and `TableVarColumn.Inherit(int)`.

`docs/migration-notes.md` carries the entry.

## Where this departed from the ticket, and why

The ticket's census of the second group was partial - it said "among them" - and three of its judgements do not survive reading the sites. **A reviewer should check these first.**

- **The ticket's list of "no engine, never catalogued" sites named `Compiler.cs:4563`. That site is `CompileRepresentationSelector`,** which builds the selector operator of a representation and whose caller does `plan.PlanCatalog.Add(representation.Selector)`. It allocates. The same holds for the sites the ticket did not mention at all: the property read and write accessors, the `IsSpecial` operator, special selectors and comparers, and the generated `iEqual`/`iCompare` operators of a scalar type (`Compiler.cs` lines 4624, 4670, 5008, 5097, 5132, 5194, 5230, 5264) - every one is added to `PlanCatalog` a few lines later. `Schema.Catalog.Validate` asserts `ID > 0` and `USEOBJECTVALIDATE` **is** defined for `DAE.csproj` in both configurations, so leaving any of them unnumbered would have failed at run time.
- **The ticket said `NativeMarshal.cs` was a marshalling stand-in with no engine. It has a `Plan`** (`NativeTableToTableVar(Plan plan, ...)`), numbers its keys from it and calls `Compiler.EnsureKey(plan, ...)` on the result, so its table variable allocates too. `ObjectMarshal.cs` is the genuine case - only an `IValueManager` is in reach - and is the one that passes `UnassignedID`.
- **The ticket said the nine `Schema.SessionObject` sites "must get a real identifier, because the object they create is registered in the catalog". A session object is not registered in any catalog.** It is a session-local-name-to-global-name map held in `ServerSession.SessionObjects` or `Plan.PlanSessionObjects`, resolved only by name; nothing in the tree reads its `ID` (checked every use of both collections), `Schema.Catalog.Validate` would reject one, and it is never persisted. Numbering them would have meant adding a public `SessionObject(int, string, string)` constructor to carry a number nobody reads. They carry `UnassignedID`, and the reason is on the type. This is the one departure a reviewer might reasonably reverse; doing so costs two constructors and one identifier per session-object-creating statement.

Three groups of sites the ticket did not mention at all, found by enumerating every `Schema.Object` descendant with a name-only constructor rather than by the ticket's grep:

- **`Schema.Column`** - hundreds of sites, 151 in `Runtime.Instructions/CatalogNodes.cs` alone. A column of a row or table type is never catalogued, never persisted and its `ID` is never read, so all of them are now unnumbered and no site needed editing. **This is the single largest change in identifier consumption in the whole chain** and it is what moved the engine's own start-up from several hundred identifiers to a few dozen.
- **`Schema.TableVarColumn`** - unnumbered is right for the columns of an intermediate result, but a column's identifier *is* persisted, inside the creation script of the table variable that holds it (`TableVarColumn.EmitStatement(ForStorage)` calls `SaveObjectID`). So the two paths whose column copies land on a table or a view had to be numbered: `Schema.TableVar.CopyTableVar` (which already had the plan, for the keys and orders) now calls `column.Inherit(plan.GetNextObjectID())` / `column.Copy(plan.GetNextObjectID())`, and the `create table T from <expression>` and `create view` paths go through it. The other callers of `Copy()`/`Inherit()` are relational operator inference onto result table variables and stay unnumbered.
- **The SQL device's reconciliation**, which creates a server table variable from a device table and inserts it into the catalog (`Device.CreateDeviceTableInServer` -> `InsertCatalogObject`). Its table variable, its columns and its references all allocate now (`SQLDevice.cs` 2408, around 2443, and 2679). **The suite does not reach this path** - it needs a real SQL Server - so it is reasoned, not observed. See the gaps below.

## The suite failure this change produced, and what it turned out to be

`AbandonedClientProcessTest.AKilledClientProcessIsReclaimedAndTheServerKeepsServing` went red, deterministically, with `An item with the same key has already been added. Key: 5` out of `CatalogDeviceSession.CacheCatalogObject` - the failure `bug-client-catalog-rebuild-reuses-object-ids` has on record at `Key: 984`.

It was traced to the end with temporary instrumentation on `ObjectIDGenerator.Reset`, `SetLastAllocated` and the duplicate-key branch (since removed; the tree has no instrumentation left). The mechanism, in order: one `Reset` from the first `Engine.InitializeCatalog`; the engine numbering its own catalog, `.System.Temp` coming out as 5; then `SETLAST from 358 to 1` - `TestEnvironment.WithTheObjectIDCounterRestored` putting the process-wide counter back to the value it saved, **while the `LocalServer` engine started inside its body was still running**; then that engine allocating 5 a second time. The object's name, `System.iCompare0000000005`, carries the identifier because `Schema.Operator.Name` is generated from it.

So the fixture's own workaround for the process-wide counter was handing a live engine identifiers its catalog already held. Before this commit that was latent: the engine spent several hundred identifiers on start-up, so its catalog objects sat well above the value the fixture rewound to. **The fix is in the fixture**: `WithTheObjectIDCounterRestored` now restores the counter only when that would *raise* it, which is all it can safely do, and is a no-op in the failing case. No production code was changed for it. `bug-object-id-counter-is-process-wide` deletes the workaround outright.

The measured mechanism is appended as an arm to `tickets/fix/bug-client-catalog-rebuild-reuses-object-ids.md`, including the one thing it does **not** settle: that ticket's reproduction needs eight table reads and a schema change and this instance needed neither, so they may be one mechanism or two, and the fixture change may have removed the recorded reproduction. That ticket is told to rebuild its reproduction first and check.

## What to exercise

Most of this is covered by the existing suite, because every relational operator builds a result table variable and most coverage scripts create catalog objects. What is worth aiming at deliberately:

- **A table or a view created from an expression.** `create table T from <expression>` and `create view V <expression>` both go through `CopyTableVar`, which is where a column copy that is going to be persisted gets numbered. Script the table, restart the server against its own store, and check the table and its columns come back - an unnumbered column would fail with `SchemaException` 113249 at `SaveObjectID` when the creation script is written.
- **Session tables, views, operators, constraints and references** (`create session table ...`). These are the nine `SessionObject` sites that deliberately carry no identifier: creating, resolving and dropping each kind of session object, and the same name as a session object and a global object at once.
- **A scalar type with a representation, properties, specials and an `iEqual`/`iCompare` pair**, created and then dropped and recreated, with the server restarted in between. That exercises every generated-operator site the ticket mis-classified, and `Schema.Catalog.Validate`'s `ID > 0` assertion fires in both configurations if one of them is unnumbered.
- **A reference with update and delete actions** (`cascade`, `clear`, `set`), which is what builds the event handlers and their operators at `Compiler.cs` 8763-8980, and the transition constraints at 7528 and 7768.
- **Alter table add column, alter operator, alter constraint** - the three `DDLNodes` stand-ins that are pushed as creation objects and now carry `UnassignedID`. If one of those were wrong, the stand-in would be rejected when something tried to cache it.
- **An engine numbers its built-in objects identically on every start.** `StoreRebuildWorkflowTest.RebuildRegistersLibrariesAndSurvivesRestart` is the existing check and it passes; it is the property that matters now that the numbering has moved.
- **The SQL device reconciling a device table into the server catalog**, against a real SQL Server. Not reachable from the suite; see below.

## Known gaps, honestly

- **The SQL device reconciliation path is unverified.** Three sites there now allocate (`SQLDevice.cs` 2408, around 2443, and 2679) because `Device.GetServerCatalog` does `Catalog.Add(tableVar)` and `CreateDeviceTableInServer` ends in `InsertCatalogObject`. The suite has no SQL Server, the spike does not reconcile, and nothing in the coverage scripts reaches it. It compiles and the reasoning is written at the sites, but it has not been run.
- **`Compiler.cs:5304` was not touched.** It is inside `#if USETYPEINHERITANCE`, which nothing defines, and it adds its operator to `PlanCatalog` - so if that constant were ever defined, that operator would be unnumbered and `Catalog.Validate` would reject it. The ticket listed the site; after this change it satisfies the ticket's letter (it does come out with `UnassignedID`) but it would be wrong if it ever compiled. Left alone rather than edited blind, because no build can check an edit to it.
- **`tests/Dataphor.Benchmarks/BenchmarkServerInstance.cs` still has the unguarded save-and-restore** that was just fixed in `TestEnvironment.cs`. It is in a project no solution builds, so it was left for `bug-object-id-counter-is-process-wide`, which deletes both.
- **No test was added.** Deliberate, and the one judgement in this handoff most worth arguing with. This step changes no behaviour on any engine path - every converted site drew from the same generator before and after - and the objects it stops numbering are exercised by a large share of the coverage scripts already. The project's rule that a behaviour port owes a characterization test is met where behaviour actually changes, in `bug-object-id-counter-is-process-wide`. The one new *rule* that could be pinned - that an object with no engine in reach carries no identifier - is an implementation choice rather than a contract, and the three guards from `engine-object-id-generator` already enforce the half that matters. The fixture fix is a test-infrastructure change whose reproduction is the suite case that found it, which now passes.
- **A site on a path the suite never persists could be unnumbered and nothing would notice.** By the design that is harmless; it is unproven rather than proven. The guards (`Schema.Object.SaveObjectID`, `ServerCatalogDeviceSession.InternalInsertPersistentObject`, `CatalogDeviceSession.CacheCatalogObject`) plus `Schema.Catalog.Validate`'s `ID > 0` are the net.

## Tripwires parked at their sites: none new

The one conditional concern in this area - that an engine spends catalog identifiers on compile-time objects no catalog will hold, so a long-lived engine moves towards `ObjectIDsExhausted` - is already recorded at `ObjectIDGenerator.Allocate` and weighed in `bug-object-id-counter-is-process-wide`. This step reduces that consumption by a large factor rather than adding to it, because a column of a row or table type no longer takes an identifier.

## What the last ticket in the chain needs to know

`bug-object-id-counter-is-process-wide` is next.

- The shared generator is no longer load-bearing: nothing in `src` or `libraries` calls the `Schema.Object` statics any more. Flipping `Engine._objectIDGenerator` to `new Schema.ObjectIDGenerator()` and deleting the three statics, `ObjectIDGenerator.SetLastAllocated` and both test workarounds is now a self-contained change.
- `ObjectIDGenerator.SetLastAllocated` carries a `NOTE:` saying it goes when `Schema.Object.SetNextObjectID` goes. Both go together.
- The numbering of built-in objects has moved a long way down (an engine's start-up now spends a few dozen identifiers, not several hundred), which is why the per-engine generator matters more than it looks: identifier reuse that used to need a thousand allocations to collide now collides within five. A catalog store written by an earlier build of 4.0 does not line up with one built from this commit and must be rebuilt from its scripts; the migration note for that consequence is already written, under this chain's two entries.
